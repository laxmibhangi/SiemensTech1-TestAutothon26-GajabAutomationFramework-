## 1. Product and user understanding

Gajab is an Indian C2C/B2C marketplace whose differentiator is **price negotiation**.
Instead of a fixed-price cart, a buyer opens a product, makes an offer, and a seller
(or a seller-side pricing engine) counters. The buyer may accept or keep bargaining.

Verified characteristics of the product:

| Aspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Web stack | Next.js / React SSR + client hydration |
| Android app | **Flutter release build** (`libflutter.so`), backend `stg.gajab.com` |
| Auth | Mobile number + 6-digit OTP (staging default `123456`); no password |
| Payments | Razorpay (`com.razorpay.CheckoutActivity`), Net Banking / UPI / cards; **no COD** |
| Localisation | English and Hinglish switcher |
| Delivery | Pincode-scoped catalogue and serviceability |
| Engagement | Live-order ticker, "Times Bargained" counters, Deal of the Day, Spin the Wheel |
| Third parties | Firebase, CleverTap, Adjust, Facebook SDK, Google Maps |

**Commercial model insight that drives risk:** revenue depends on the bargaining engine
producing a price that is both attractive to the buyer and profitable to the seller.
Defects in bargaining logic are therefore *financial* defects, not merely functional ones.

## 2. Testing objectives

1. Verify the end-to-end buyer journey from discovery through bargaining to order
   confirmation works on supported browsers and on Android.
2. Confirm the **bargaining engine** behaves correctly, deterministically and within
   sane commercial bounds.
3. Confirm **money is always correct** — asking price, offer, accepted price, payable
   amount and recorded savings must reconcile.
4. Assess trust signals (live orders, bargain counts, social proof) for accuracy.
5. Assess accessibility, performance, security and privacy at a level proportionate to
   an e-commerce product handling payments and personal data.
6. Produce evidence-based, reproducible defects ranked by business impact.

---

## 3. Scope

### In scope
- Guest browsing: home widgets, category navigation, listing, filters, search
- Authentication: OTP request, verification, session establishment
- Pincode / delivery-location selection and its effect on catalogue
- Product detail page and the bargain modal (offer, counter, accept, re-bargain)
- Checkout: address creation, bill summary, payment method selection
- Order placement and post-purchase views (My Bargains, order confirmation)
- Language switching (English / Hinglish)
- Cross-browser: Chrome, Firefox, Edge
- Android app on a physical device
- Non-functional: accessibility, front-end performance, client-side security, visual

### Out of scope
- Production environment (explicitly prohibited)
- Seller/admin portals and seller KYC
- Razorpay's internal processing (third-party; we verify our integration only)
- Load, stress and DoS testing (prohibited by the rules of engagement)
- Backend/database and infrastructure testing
- iOS application
- Email/SMS gateway deliverability beyond observable behaviour

## 4. Assumptions and dependencies

**Assumptions**
- Staging data is representative enough to exercise real journeys.
- The default OTP `123456` is valid for all staging accounts.
- Placing orders on staging is expected and carries no financial consequence.
- Seller counter-offers on staging are generated by the same engine as production.

**Dependencies**
- Staging availability. *Observed: intermittent slowness and `INTERNAL_SERVER_ERROR`
  responses during the session.*
- An authorised test mobile number (held in `.env`, never committed).
- A physical Android device — the corporate laptop has Hyper-V/VBS active and
  `HypervisorPlatform` disabled, so the Android emulator cannot boot.
- SMTP credentials for the "email the deal" step.

## 5. Risk assessment

Risk = business impact × likelihood. Testing effort is allocated in this order.

| # | Risk | Impact | Likelihood | Priority | Evidence / rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Bargaining engine accepts a loss-making or absurd price | Critical | Medium | **P0** | A product was listed at **₹1.00**; bargaining could compound mispricing |
| R2 | Payment/amount mismatch between accepted offer and charged amount | Critical | Medium | **P0** | Bargain → checkout hand-off recalculates the payable amount |
| R3 | Login unreachable or unreliable | Critical | Medium | **P0** | OTP screen auto-submits with no visible confirmation; silent failure if terms unticked |
| R4 | Order placed but not recorded (or vice versa) | Critical | Low | **P0** | Payment callback handling |
| R5 | Checkout address form loses data and blocks purchase | High | High | **P1** | Reproduced: pincode lookup re-renders the form and clears `address1` |
| R6 | Misleading trust signals (bargain counts, live orders) | High | High | **P1** | "Bought by N people" changed 491 → 999 for the same SKU on re-render |
| R7 | Accessibility barriers | High | High | **P1** | ~60 duplicate DOM IDs; `live-order-message-price-1` repeated **37×** |
| R8 | Client-side hydration instability | High | High | **P1** | React **error #418** on sign-in; controlled inputs discard state |
| R9 | Pincode/serviceability returns wrong locations | Medium | High | **P2** | `560001` (Bengaluru) suggested a **West Bengal** address |
| R10 | PII/OTP leakage in logs, URLs or third-party SDKs | High | Low | **P2** | Five analytics SDKs present |
| R11 | Performance degradation on the home page | Medium | High | **P2** | Never-idle page; preloaded assets unused; Google Maps loaded without `async` |
| R12 | Cross-browser rendering/behaviour differences | Medium | Medium | **P2** | Tailwind + heavy client rendering |
| R13 | Android app parity with web | Medium | Medium | **P3** | Flutter canvas rendering |

## 8. Negative and boundary scenarios

**Input boundaries**
- Mobile: 9 / 10 / 11 digits, alphabetic, `+91` prefix, leading zeros, whitespace
- OTP: 5 / 6 / 7 digits, alphabetic, expired, reused
- Pincode: 5 / 6 / 7 digits, non-numeric, unserviceable
- Offer: `0`, `-100`, `0.001`, `999999999`, scientific notation, `1e5`

**State and flow**
- Back-navigation mid-bargain and mid-payment
- Reload during the bargain modal
- Session expiry between accepting an offer and paying
- Double-clicking Pay / Buy Now (duplicate order risk)
- Two tabs bargaining the same SKU

**Security-oriented**
- XSS payloads in address fields (`<script>alert(1)</script>`)
- Tampering with the offer amount client-side before submission
- Direct navigation to `/checkout` without an accepted bargain
- Accessing another user's order/bargain by ID manipulation

## 10. Accessibility considerations

Standard: **WCAG 2.1 AA**. Tooling: axe-core, keyboard-only traversal, screen reader.

Focus areas:
- **Duplicate DOM IDs** — a W3C violation that breaks `label[for]`, `aria-labelledby`
  and screen-reader navigation. Already observed at scale.
- Hidden-input checkbox pattern (`opacity-0`) used for Terms and brand filters — must
  remain reachable and announced.
- Keyboard operability of the bargain modal, including focus trapping and Escape.
- The coach-mark overlay has **no close button** and no keyboard dismissal.
- Colour contrast on promotional gradients.
- Continuously animating live-order ticker — needs a `prefers-reduced-motion` path.
- Meaningful `alt` text (several product images reuse an unrelated `alt`).

---

## 11. Performance considerations

Tooling: Lighthouse, Chrome DevTools, WebPageTest, Network throttling.

| Concern | Observation |
|---|---|
| Page never reaches idle | Load event effectively never settles; polling + animation keep the main thread busy |
| Wasted bandwidth | Multiple `preload`ed assets never used within seconds of load |
| Third-party cost | Google Maps loaded **without `loading=async`**; five analytics SDKs |
| Image weight | Large hero/texture PNGs (up to ~1.9 MB in the app bundle) |
| Perceived performance | Hydration mismatch causes visible re-render churn |

Targets: LCP < 2.5 s, CLS < 0.1, TBT < 300 ms on a mid-tier device over 4G.

---

## 12. Security and privacy considerations

Non-intrusive, read-only assessment only — no DoS, no privilege escalation, no data
extraction, in line with the rules of engagement.

- OTP must never appear in the DOM, URLs, console or analytics payloads.
- Verify the offer amount is validated **server-side**, not just in the browser.
- Check authorisation on order/bargain endpoints (IDOR).
- Confirm HTTPS everywhere, secure cookie flags, and no secrets in client bundles.
- Review what PII the five third-party SDKs receive (phone, address, location).
- Confirm the address form escapes user input on render (stored XSS).
- Payment fields must be gateway-hosted, never handled by Gajab's own DOM.

## 14. Entry and exit criteria

**Entry**
- Staging reachable and functionally stable
- Authorised test account available
- APK installed on a physical Android device
- Framework dependencies installed and smoke test green

**Exit**
- All P0 scenarios executed
- No open Critical/Blocker defects in the critical journey
- Cross-browser E2E executed on Chrome, Firefox and Edge
- Android smoke executed on a real device
- Accessibility, performance and security checks completed with evidence
- All defects logged with reproduction steps and screenshots
- Reports and AI-usage disclosure produced

---

## 15. Execution approach

1. **Risk-based, journey-first.** Exercise CJ1–CJ4 before breadth.
2. **Exploratory charters** (time-boxed to 30 min) on bargaining, checkout and pincode —
   the areas where automation is weakest and money is at stake.
3. **Automated regression** (Python + Selenium + pytest) for the 22-step journey, run
   across three browsers.
4. **Android** via Appium on a physical device for the critical journey.
5. **Tooling passes**: axe-core, Lighthouse, DevTools console/network review.
6. **Triage and evidence**: every defect gets steps, expected/actual, severity,
   business impact and a screenshot or console capture.

**Prioritisation:** P0 = money, auth or data-loss risk on the critical journey.
P1 = high-frequency journeys, trust and accessibility. P2 = secondary flows and
cosmetic-but-visible issues. P3 = edge cases. Within a band, order by
*likelihood × user reach*.

---

## 16. Known limitations

- Staging data differs from the brief; some named products/brands are absent.
- Only one authorised test account, so multi-user and concurrency testing is limited.
- The Android emulator is unavailable (corporate VBS policy); a physical device is
  required, restricting device/OS matrix breadth.
- Razorpay behaviour on staging may not mirror production.
- No backend/log access, so defects are diagnosed from client-side evidence only.
- Load and stress testing are prohibited, so performance findings are single-user.
- Time-boxed to the hackathon window; coverage is deliberately depth-over-breadth on
  the critical journey.
- Live counters change continuously, so some assertions verify *plausibility and
  stability* rather than exact values.

## 17. AI usage

AI (GitHub Copilot) was used to accelerate: framework scaffolding, locator discovery
from captured DOM snapshots, diagnosis of React-hydration failures, and drafting of this
strategy. Every AI-produced locator and workaround was **executed against staging and
verified**; several initial AI assumptions were wrong and were corrected by observation
— notably an early claim that the Terms checkbox was permanently broken, which manual
testing disproved. No credentials, OTPs or personal data were shared with AI tooling.
